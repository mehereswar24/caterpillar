CAT 320 EXCAVATOR — EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

MACHINE FIRE
If smoke or fire is visible from the engine compartment: do not open the engine hood. Press the emergency stop button. Operate the cab-mounted fire suppression system if fitted. Exit the cab immediately using the primary exit door. Do not jump — use steps and handholds. Move at least 50 metres upwind from the machine. Call emergency services. Use a portable fire extinguisher on small fires only — aim at the base of the flame, not the top. Never re-enter a burning machine. After a fire, the machine must be inspected by a CAT technician before restarting.

MACHINE OVERTURN / ROLLOVER
If the machine begins to roll over: grip the steering wheel or handrail firmly. Do not attempt to jump from a rolling machine — the machine will roll over an ejected operator. Stay in the cab — the ROPS structure will protect you if the seatbelt is fastened. Brace your feet on the floor and turn your face away from the side of impact. After the machine comes to rest: assess yourself for injury. Turn off the engine if possible. Exit through the highest available door or window. Call emergency services.

HYDRAULIC INJECTION INJURY
High-pressure hydraulic fluid injected into the skin appears as a small entry wound but spreads widely under the skin and is immediately life-threatening. Even low-pressure fluid at 100 bar can penetrate deeply. Do not treat as a puncture wound. Go to emergency department immediately — tell the doctor it is a hydraulic injection injury and state the hydraulic system pressure. Surgery is usually required within hours to prevent tissue death, amputation, or death from infection.

WORKING NEAR LIVE POWER LINES — CONTACT
If the machine contacts a live overhead power line: stay in the cab — the machine provides ground insulation. Do not touch any metal part of the machine interior other than controls. Call out to ground workers to stay back at least 20 metres. Sound the horn continuously. Call emergency services using hands-free phone or radio. Do not exit the cab until the power authority confirms the line is de-energised. If the machine is on fire and you must exit: jump — do not step — as far from the machine as possible. Land with both feet together. Shuffle away with feet together to avoid step potential gradient. Do not run until at least 20 metres from the machine.

MACHINE SINKING IN SOFT GROUND
If the machine begins to sink: move forward or backward immediately to distribute weight. Lower the boom to the ground and use it as an outrigger — push against the ground to lift the tracks. If the machine is stuck: stop all travel movement — spinning tracks dig deeper. Call for a recovery excavator with appropriate ground pressure equipment. Never attach a recovery cable to the bucket or boom — use only designated tow points at the undercarriage.

GAS LINE STRIKE
If you suspect you have struck a gas main: stop all excavation immediately. Turn off the engine if it is not inside the potential gas cloud. Do not use any electrical switch — a spark could ignite gas. Do not use radio or phone near the suspected strike. Clear all personnel from a minimum 50-metre radius. Call emergency services from outside the exclusion zone. Wait for the gas company to arrive and confirm it is safe before returning to the area.

WORKER STRUCK BY MACHINE
Stop all machine movement immediately. Sound the horn to alert others. Do not move the machine — it may be providing pressure preventing additional blood loss. Call emergency services. Do not move the injured person unless they are in immediate danger from fire, flooding, or further machine movement. Assign one person to stay with the injured. Assign another to direct emergency services to the location. Do not clean or disturb the site until investigators have documented the scene.

FIRST AID ESSENTIALS FOR SITE OPERATORS
Crush injury: do not remove a heavy object from a crushed limb without medical supervision — sudden release of pressure can cause crush syndrome and cardiac arrest. Call emergency services before any attempt to free the person.
Electrocution: do not touch the victim until you are certain the power source is disconnected. Use a non-conducting object (dry wood, rubber mat) to separate victim from power source if it cannot be switched off. Start CPR immediately if trained.
Heat stroke: cool the victim rapidly — remove outer clothing, apply cool water to the skin, fan continuously, apply ice packs to armpits and groin. This is the most critical action and must not be delayed while waiting for the ambulance.
Severe bleeding: apply firm direct pressure with a clean pad — maintain pressure for at least 10 minutes without lifting. For limb bleeding, apply a tourniquet 5-7 cm above the wound. Note the time of tourniquet application — tell medics on arrival.
