CAT 320 EXCAVATOR — SAFETY MANUAL

PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE)
Always wear a hard hat when outside the cab on a construction site. High-visibility vest is mandatory at all times outside the cab. Steel-toed safety boots must be worn — no exceptions. Safety glasses are required when working around dust, flying debris, or overhead work. Hearing protection must be worn when exposed to noise above 85 dB for more than 2 hours — cab exterior noise is 102 dB(A). Gloves are required when handling attachments, hydraulic hoses, or hot components. Never wear loose clothing, scarves, or jewellery that could catch on controls or machinery.

MOUNTING AND DISMOUNTING
Always use the three-point contact rule when climbing on or off the machine — two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand in contact with the machine at all times. Never jump from the machine — fall injuries are the most common excavator accidents. Face the machine when climbing up or down. Never mount or dismount a moving machine. Do not carry tools or objects in your hands while climbing — use a tool bag attached to your belt. Clean mud and grease from steps and handholds before climbing.

SEATBELT COMPLIANCE
The seatbelt must be fastened at all times when the engine is running. The ROPS (Roll-Over Protective Structure) only protects the operator when the seatbelt is fastened. If the machine tips over and the seatbelt is not fastened, the operator will be thrown against the cab structure or ejected. Check seatbelt webbing and buckle before every shift — replace if any damage is found. The seatbelt warning light will illuminate if the seatbelt is not fastened within 3 seconds of the engine starting.

EXCLUSION ZONES AND PROXIMITY SAFETY
Establish a minimum 5-metre exclusion zone around the machine during all operations. Post spotter at all access points to the exclusion zone. If a worker must enter the zone, the operator must park the machine, lower all attachments to the ground, shut down the engine, and confirm eye contact with the worker before they approach. When using a spotter, agree on hand signals before starting work. Sound the horn twice before swinging or travelling. Use the reverse travel alarm — test it before every shift. When working on a road, cone off a minimum 10 metres in each direction from the machine. Never allow anyone to stand or walk under a raised load or raised attachment.

SLOPE AND STABILITY SAFETY
Do not operate on slopes greater than 35 degrees (70% grade) — this is the maximum gradeability of the machine. For safe sustained work, limit slopes to 15 degrees (27% grade). Always travel up and down slopes, never across them — side-slope travel is the primary cause of rollover. When travelling down a slope, use low travel speed and let the machine brake itself — do not allow the machine to freewheel. If the machine begins to slip sideways on a slope, lower the boom and bucket to the ground immediately to stabilise. Never swing a load sideways on a slope. On soft ground or near excavation edges, maintain a minimum 1.5 x depth setback from the edge — example: 3 metre deep trench requires machine to be 4.5 metres from edge. Always check ground bearing capacity before driving onto new areas. On frozen ground, be aware that thawing can cause sudden loss of bearing capacity.

WORKING NEAR UTILITIES
Always call the utility location service before any excavation — in most countries this is legally required. Maintain minimum 1 metre clearance from marked utility lines at all times. When within 3 metres of a utility, switch to hand digging only. If you strike an underground utility, stop work immediately. If you strike a gas line, do not use any electrical equipment, do not start the engine if it is off, evacuate the area and call emergency services. If you strike a power cable and the machine is energised, stay in the cab — the cab provides protection. Sound the horn and warn others to stay away. Call emergency services and wait for the power to be switched off before exiting.

FIRE PREVENTION AND RESPONSE
Carry a minimum 9 kg dry powder fire extinguisher in the cab — check monthly. Inspect the engine bay daily for oil or fuel leaks on hot components — these are fire ignition sources. Clean up any spilled fuel before starting the engine. Keep the engine compartment free of accumulated debris — dry grass and leaves are serious fire hazards. If a fire starts in the engine compartment, shut down the engine, exit the cab, and use the extinguisher from a safe distance targeting the base of the fire. If the fire is too large to control, move at least 50 metres upwind and call emergency services.

WORKING AT HEIGHT AND OVERHEAD HAZARDS
Check overhead clearance before raising the boom — minimum 6 metre clearance below power lines is required by law in most jurisdictions (maintain 10 metres for safety). If overhead power lines are present, appoint a dedicated spotter whose only job is to watch the clearance. Never assume power lines are de-energised — treat all lines as live. When loading trucks, never swing the load over the cab of the truck or truck driver. Confirm the truck driver is out of the cab or the cab is fitted with overhead protection before loading.

PEDESTRIAN MANAGEMENT
Hold daily site safety briefings identifying the excavation zone and pedestrian exclusion areas. Use physical barriers such as fence panels or water-filled barriers to separate pedestrian paths from machine operating areas. Use a traffic management plan for sites with road access. The operator should never rely solely on the camera system — always physically check blind spots before moving. When working in areas where pedestrians may be present, assign a dedicated ground-worker who communicates by radio with the operator.

NIGHT OPERATIONS
The machine must be equipped with all working lights operational. Minimum lighting: cab interior light, front boom light, rear travel light. Recommended additional lighting: 360-degree LED light bar. Operators working night shifts must be rested — minimum 8 hours sleep before shift. Visibility in the camera systems is reduced at night — use extra caution. Reflective PPE is mandatory for all ground workers during night operations. Reduce travel speed by 30% in poorly lit areas.

EMERGENCY SHUTDOWN PROCEDURE
Red emergency stop button is located on the right console inside the cab. This cuts all electrical power and hydraulics immediately. Use only in life-threatening emergencies as it may damage hydraulic components. Normal shutdown: lower all attachments to the ground, move all controls to neutral, engage parking brake, reduce throttle to idle, wait 5 minutes, turn key to off.
